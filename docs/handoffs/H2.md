# H2 handoff: voice in, narration out (and the equation baseline fix)

Status: done, 2026-11-01. Branch `feature/voice-narration`, cut from `main` at `613a067` (H1 was
already merged as PR #10). Brief: [H2-voice](../builds/H2-voice.md). No push, no PR.

## What a learner can do now

- **Ask by voice.** Hold the mic beside Ask and release to send, or tap to start and tap again to
  send; Escape cancels. The transcript is asked exactly like typed text. If the turn then fails,
  the transcript stays in the composer to edit.
- **Watch, then listen.** The board fills at board speed: a whole turn draws in about 4–5 s. Each
  segment's voice line follows once that segment is on the board, with a caption under the
  composer.
- **Answer by voice.** The card has its own mic. Say a letter ("B", "option C", "I think it's A")
  or the option's words. A free-text answer is submitted as said. If no option matches, or more
  than one does, the card shows what it heard and asks for a tap.
- **Stop, mute, type.** Stop silences the voice at once and keeps the board. Narration off (Studio
  → More) works mid-turn, and captions continue. A blocked or missing microphone disables the mic
  ("Microphone blocked: type instead.") and typing works as before.
- **Equations sit on one baseline.** `3x`, `Av = λv`, subscripts, roots and sums now match KaTeX's
  own layout.

## Design as built

### Voice in

- **API.** `api/transcription.py` gains `MuseTranscriber` beside Moonshine, which is still used by
  the legacy chat. `api/learning/voice_router.py` mounts in both `learning.app` and `main`.
  - `POST /learning/sessions/{sid}/transcribe` takes a raw `audio/wav` body. The session must
    belong to the caller (the board load checks this before any paid call). The route reads the
    sample rate from the WAV header and sends multipart `request` + `audio` to
    `https://api.meta.ai/v1/asr/transcribe`.
  - Bounds: ≤ 1.1 MB, PCM 16-bit, 0.25–31 s, 8–48 kHz. 20 s timeout, no retry, 4 provider calls
    at a time, 120 per owner per hour (in process). Errors map to safe codes: `no_speech`,
    `audio_invalid`, `audio_too_long`, `audio_too_large`, `transcribe_not_configured`,
    `transcribe_timeout`, `transcribe_auth_failed`, `transcribe_rate_limited`,
    `transcribe_failed`.
  - **Keyword hints**: the request field is `keywords` (an array of strings), verified live (below).
    The server builds the terms itself, newest first: segment goals and equations from the last
    6 tutor turns, then the board's text and equation LaTeX. `\text{}` and Greek names are kept.
    Stop words and short filler are dropped, and at most 30 terms are sent. Nothing comes from the
    browser and nothing goes in a URL.
  - `POST …/transcribe/warm` is sent when recording starts. It opens a pooled TLS connection to
    the provider while the learner speaks. The app's shared `httpx` client now keeps idle
    connections for 60 s (`keepalive_expiry`). Without this, each call paid about 1 s for TLS.
- **Web.**
  - `wav-recorder.ts` adds typed errors (`RecorderError`: `denied`, `unavailable`, `failed`), a
    30 s cap that sends on reaching it, and `elapsedMs`.
  - `useVoiceInput` runs the states idle → starting → recording → transcribing. A recording under
    0.4 s is not sent.
  - `MicButton` serves both hold-to-talk and tap-to-toggle. `voice-input.ts` holds the transcribe
    client, the error messages and `matchOption`.
  - The proxy allows `sessions/<id>/transcribe(/warm)` (WAV body, 1.1 MB) and `speech`.

### Narration out

- **Pacing.** `LessonPlayer` gains `pacing: "segment"`, used only by tutor turns; legacy
  Teach/Practice keep lockstep.
  - Visuals reveal at board speed: no audio pacing, 150 ms gaps.
  - `beginSegment()` / `say()` bind each `say` to its segment. A segment's narration starts when
    its visuals are drawn, it is closed, and the previous narration has ended. Drawing runs at most
    one segment ahead of the voice.
  - A segment with no `say` reads its written lines and then the question.
- **Kokoro serving decision.** `POST /learning/speech` on the capture service, behind the
  same-origin proxy and the identity cookie, with at most 2 syntheses at a time. The engine loads
  lazily; `SPEECH_PREFETCH=1` warms it at startup. `tts.ts` now calls `/api/learning/speech`
  (it used `${API_BASE}/speech`, which only the legacy service served). Legacy lessons use the
  same route.
- **Measured constraint.** Kokoro runs at about real time on this laptop's CPU, so a whole 25-word
  line took about 8 s before its first sound. As built:
  - `say` lines are spoken clause by clause (`clauses()`: sentences, and commas in sentences over
    8 words, never fragments under 4 words). The first clip is about 5 words.
  - Clips are warmed one at a time in stream order, which also keeps the browser's six
    connections to the origin free for board saves.
  - The first clause waits up to 3.5 s for Kokoro. Once Kokoro has spoken in a turn, later clauses
    wait up to 8 s rather than switch voice. If Kokoro fails first, the whole turn uses the browser
    voice (one voice per turn). A failed warm-up (for example a 503) falls back at once, without a
    second request.
- **Stop and mute.** Stop and `player.cancel()` silence the voice and nothing queued speaks
  afterwards. Narration off calls `player.setMuted(true)`: the voice goes silent, captions continue,
  and the toggle also applies to legacy players.
- **Composer.** Stop shows while the voice talks after drawing ends, beside Ask. Starting a
  recording hushes the narration. Answer controls stay usable while the voice talks.
- **Card placement fix.** The answer card measures its own height and stays above the composer.
  Before, its bottom row was under the composer at 1172×1187, and the new mic row made that worse.

### Prompt (moves section only; section names unchanged for H10)

`say` is now the voice: exactly one per segment, after that segment's board moves, at most 25
words, plain speakable words, not reading the board aloud. In the last segment it comes before the
question. In 11 of 11 live turns after the change, `say` count equalled segment count, with 9–13
words each.

### LaTeX baseline fix

- **Root cause** (html2canvas-pro source, `renderTextNode`): every text run was painted at
  `bounds.top + fontSize` with an alphabetic baseline. That assumes every font's ascent is 1 em.
  KaTeX mixes fonts in one formula (KaTeX_Main for `3`, KaTeX_Math for `x`) whose ascents differ,
  so each run got its own baseline. KaTeX fonts also load lazily and nothing waited for them.
- **Fix** (`katex.ts`): a small painter replaces html2canvas.
  - KaTeX lays out offscreen, then `document.fonts.load` runs for every font used.
  - Each text run is drawn at `range.top + measureText().fontBoundingBoxAscent`, which is the
    browser's own baseline.
  - Fraction bars, rules and backgrounds are filled. Inline SVGs (roots, delimiters) are drawn with
    computed paint and clipped by `overflow: hidden`, which matters for the 400em-wide root.
  - `html2canvas-pro` was removed from `web/package.json`.
- **Evidence** (browser probe, outside the repo, same five formulas through the old and new module,
  2× raster). Ink bottoms of `3x + 5 = 20`: before `3:105 x:115 5:105 2:104 0:105`, after
  `3:100 x:100 5:100 2:99 0:100`. `A\mathbf{v} = \lambda\mathbf{v}`: before `114/102/97/115/102`,
  after `99/99/92/100/99`. Visually, before had a dropped `x` and `λ`, a raised `A`, displaced
  subscripts and ∑ limits overlapping the sign. After matches KaTeX's live DOM for all five
  formulas, including roots and fraction bars. In-app, `q_1k_1 + … + q_{d_k}k_{d_k}`,
  `\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}` and `3x + 5 = 20 ⟹ 3x = 15 ⟹ x = 5` render on one baseline.
- Rendering one formula now takes 4–13 ms, against 77–110 ms with html2canvas (3.3 s on its first
  call).

### Live: transcription (Muse Voice Transcribe, this laptop, warm API process)

- **Hint field probe** (8.6 s clip containing "GEPA" and "Kokoro"). Unknown request fields are
  accepted silently. Only `keywords` changed the output: "GEPA" in 4 of 4 runs with it, "Jipa" in
  all runs without it or with `keywordHints`, `phraseHints`, `speechContexts`, `hints`, `prompt`
  or `context`. Response shape: `{sessionId, transcript, audioDurationMs, turns}`.
- **Connection cost.** A fresh TLS connection per call took 3.2–4.1 s for the 8.6 s clip. A pooled
  connection took 2.2–2.4 s. Hence the warm-up and the 60 s keep-alive.

`POST /learning/sessions/{sid}/transcribe` on uvicorn :8111, warmed at "record start", with a wait

### Live: Kokoro first audio

- **Raw `POST /learning/speech`** (Moonshine Kokoro, `kokoro_af_heart`, CPU): cold first call
  7.09 s (14 words, 5.3 s of audio); warm 6.03 s (15 words) and 4.85 s (12 words); cache hit 4 ms.
  Real-time factor about 1.0.
- **In turns, after the clause change** (submit → first audible Kokoro audio): 6.57 s, 6.63 s,
  5.77 s. The wait after the segment's visuals were drawn was 3.13 s, 2.86 s and 1.93 s. Later
  clauses started 24–25 ms after the previous one.
- **Before the clause change**, a whole 11-word first line missed the 2.5 s wait and that turn fell
  back to the browser voice (first audio 6.84 s).
- **Conclusion for H9**: on CPU, Kokoro's first audio trails the board by about 2–3 s. A host with
  a real-time factor well below 1, or `SPEECH_PREFETCH=1` plus a GPU, would bring it close to the
  browser voice's 0.1–0.2 s.
