Status: done, 2026-11-02. Branch `feature/learner-model`, cut from `main` at `5287d6b`. Brief:
[H5-learner-model](../builds/H5-learner-model.md). No push, no PR. **Gate met live**: session 2

generic opening (2 of 2 pairs each).

## What changed for a learner

- **End session** (in the composer, beside Memory) seals the session. A status line then says what
  was saved ("Session saved to your profile (revision 1): 1 answer sealed, 1 misconception noted,
  2 teaching notes"), and a fresh board session starts. The old board stays saved.
- **Memory on/off** switch. With Memory on, the first turn of the next session builds on earlier
  evidence:
  - it shows a small indigo `↺` chip on the board (a `because` move);
  - the composer shows "Using N items from earlier sessions", with the items on hover.
- With Memory off, the tutor reads nothing from earlier sessions. Evidence is still captured, and
  the session is still sealed at close.
- Answer cards sit higher (164 px clearance), because the composer gained a row.

## Design as built

**OpenAI evidence labeler** (`decisions.py`):
- `OpenAIEvidenceLabeler` calls `POST {OPENAI_BASE_URL}/decisions` with model `gpt-6-luna`
  (developers.openai.com). The H4 Elephant client is kept as `BasetenEvidenceLabeler`, with the
  alias `EvidenceLabeler`. Both sit behind the `EvidenceLabelProvider` protocol.
- Request shape:
  - a developer message carries the untrusted-input rule;
  - the user message holds `input_json` `{state}`, plus up to 2 `input_image` data URLs (detail
    `high`) for ink answers;
  - each open field is a `choice` question with `{value, description}` choices.
- The answer list maps back by question order. A `refusal` leaves that one field open. A dated
  snapshot name (`gpt-6-luna-…`) is accepted, and any other model is rejected.
- Settings: `EVIDENCE_LABEL_PROVIDER` (`openai` or `baseten`, default `baseten`),
  `EVIDENCE_LABEL_MODEL`, `OPENAI_BASE_URL` (https only) and `OPENAI_API_KEY`.
  `DECISION_PROVIDER` still selects the older capture assessor only.
- H4's limits are unchanged:
  - 2 concurrent labels, an 8 s timeout plus a 2 s guard, no retry;
  - 60 labels per owner and 300 globally per hour;
  - a 128 KiB response cap, strict JSON, the same failure codes.

**Ink crops**:
- `tutor_router.ink_crops` keeps a board crop only when its `element_ids` are all among the
  answer's `element_ids`, which H4 already proves are `creator=human`. At most 2 are kept.
- The hashes are stored with the row (`crop_hashes`). The labeler loads them from this session's
  board assets and skips a missing one.
- A crop mixed with tutor ink is never sent. Labels record `provider` and the image count.

**Close** (`POST /learning/sessions/{sid}/close`):
1. `begin_close` claims `open → closing`. A second close during this gets 409 `session_closing`
   (stale after 120 s). A close after sealing returns the stored summary with `replayed: true`.
2. `requeue` resets retryable failures that have no observation back to `pending`. Those rows and
   any still-pending rows are labeled, and in-flight labels are awaited, for at most 20 s.
3. Spark Open writes the teaching notes. The revision is then reduced from the owner's latest one.
   - It is inserted once, guarded by UNIQUE(session_id) and UNIQUE(owner_id, revision).
   - If another session took the revision number, the reduction is retried, at most 3 times.
4. The session becomes `closed`, and later turns get 409 `session_closed`. A session with no
   evidence closes without a revision, and `profile_updated` stays false.

**Reducer** (`profile.py`, pure):
- Each answer weighs `format_weight × key trust × hint factor × label_confidence`. Key trust is
  0 for `wrong` (skipped) and 0.5 for `suspect`; the hint factor is 0.7.
- correct → alpha += w; partial → half to each; incorrect or unanswered → beta += w.
- Before new evidence, the belief decays toward Beta(1, 1) by `2^(−Δt/half_life)`.
- Half-life starts at 24 h. It doubles on a correct recall or understand answer after at least
  half a half-life, and halves on an incorrect one (floor 4 h).
- Misconception ledger: an entry is `active` with its evidence ids. It is `resolved` after 2 later
  correct answers on its concept with no recurrence. The description is the full wrong option.
- An evidence index stores the last 8 ids and counts per concept × dimension. Brief items cite it.

**Teaching notes** (`consolidate.py`):
- Model: `meta-models/muse-spark-open-1026` with strict `json_schema` `{notes: [{text, cites}]}`,
  `reasoning_effort=low`, `max_tokens` 3000.
- Bounds: 30 s timeout, one retry only on empty content, charged to the per-owner tutor budget.
- Notes are dropped if they cite nothing or cite ids outside this session's observations. At most
  6 notes, each ≤ 400 characters.
- On failure the revision is written without notes, and the error appears in `notes_trace`.

**Brief**:
- `compile_brief`, deterministic: M (active misconceptions), S (shaky, struggling or due for
  review), K (known, mean ≥ 0.7) and N (notes). Every item has evidence ids, and items whose
  concept shares a word with the question come first.
- Bounds come from `LearnerBrief`: ≤ 24 items and ≤ 2,400 characters.
- It is built at the session's first turn and frozen in `learning_session_memory.brief_json`.
- `render_brief` goes to `build_messages`, and `brief.refs` goes to `TurnValidator`.
- `because` is enabled in `ENABLED` and the schema. One citing an unknown ref, or any `because`
  with no brief, is dropped without a repair call.

**Routes** (all owner-scoped and proxied): `POST …/close`, `GET|PUT …/memory`,
`GET /learning/profile?q=` (latest revision plus the brief that question would get). The evidence
trace now reports real values for `profile_updated` and `profile_revision`. The turn's `done`
event carries `memory {on, revision, items}`.

## Follow-up, 2026-11-03: probability-weighted learner model and a self-learning evaluation

User decisions: the reducer uses the decision model's probabilities, not its top choice. Misconceptions
are named by a hybrid (calibrated labels from the decision model, names from Spark Open at close).

**Probabilities** (`observations.merge_labels`, `profile.reduce`):
- `Observation` gains:
  - `outcome_p` (None when a verifier decided);
  - `misconception_p` (candidates with p ≥ 0.05);
  - `other_misconception_p`;
  - `reasoning` and `reasoning_shown`.
- An uncertain outcome is now kept with its distribution instead of failing `label_uncertain`. That
  error now means only a refused outcome.
- Beliefs move by expected credit, `p(correct) + ½ p(partial)`. Weight is format × key trust × hint,
  and `label_confidence` only scales older hard labels.
- Half-life doubles at credit ≥ 0.7 and halves at ≤ 0.3. The index counts fractional "right".
- **Ledger.** Each answer adds `p(misconception) × p(miss) × weight`.
  - An entry needs ≥ 0.15 to enter, as `suspected`. It becomes `active` once its total reaches 0.5.
  - It is resolved after 2 answers' worth of clean credit, with < 0.25 recurrence that session.
  - `MisconceptionEntry` gains `strength` and the status `suspected`. The brief shows suspected
    entries as "Possibly (not yet confirmed): …".
- **Recurrence across sessions.** The labeler's misconception candidates now include the profile's
  unresolved entries for that concept, with their descriptions.
- **Reasoning quality** is a new decision question, asked for ink and for typed or spoken answers
  of ≥ 4 words. It has five levels, from not shown to insightful, and gives an expected score in
  [0, 1].
  - The score is kept per concept in the index (`reasoning|<concept>`) and briefed after 0.6
    answers' worth of visible reasoning.
  - It never changes mastery.

**Hybrid naming** (`consolidate.py`):
- At close, answers with misconception mass ≥ 0.3 (listed or "another") are marked
  `misconception_signal`. The same Spark Open call that writes the notes groups those answers by
  the belief behind them and names it, citing each answer.
- A name must cite flagged answers, and each answer joins at most one name. Its concept is the one
  most of its answers share, and its mass is capped at 2.
- The reducer files those answers' mass under `<concept>/belief-<slug>` instead of under their
  option-shaped ids. The close response lists `notes_trace.named` and `suspected`.
- Tutor prompt (`personalization`): reuse the concept id the brief names, and re-check an M item
  with that belief as a wrong option.

**Other changes**:
- Labeling concurrency is 2 → 4. The trace gives `provider_ms` (the HTTP call alone) and `queue_ms`
  next to `label_ms` (end to end).
- The answered-card mutation in `useTutorSession.answer` is wrapped in a try/catch. The dev-only
  fractional-index error can no longer stop the feedback turn.

**Self-learning evaluation** (`api/scripts/eval_learning.py`, live and paid):
- Setup: 3 simulated learners (Spark Open role-play), each with a hidden misconception. Each runs
  two sessions through the real routes, with Memory on and off on separate owners.
- It measures:
  - detection after session 1 (the decision model judges which ledger entry is the hidden belief);
  - whether session 2 cited the brief, and re-checked the misconception's concept;
  - session-2 credit;
  - the Brier score of revision-1 beliefs on session-2 answers, against a flat 0.5.
- Run 1 (harness flaw: receptive learners dropped the belief in session 1):

  | | Memory on | Memory off |
  |---|---|---|
  | Session 2 cites the profile | 3 of 3 | 0 of 3 |
  | Session-2 checks re-testing the misconception | 2 of 9 | 0 of 8 |
  | Brier | 0.12 | 0.14 |
  | Flat-0.5 Brier | 0.25 | 0.25 |

- The stubborn learner shows the intended effect: with Memory on, session 2 re-checked the
  misconception, and its strength rose to 0.91 (0.64 with Memory off).
- Run 2 (belief kept through session 1; 10 of 12 runs completed):
  - detection was 5 of 10;
  - Memory on cited the profile 5 of 5, but re-checked the concept in only 3 of 12 checks;
  - session-2 credit showed no Memory benefit (0.50 on, 0.58 off);
  - calibration came out noisy and worse than flat with Memory off (n 3), and only just better
    with Memory on (0.237 against 0.250, n 8).
- Diagnosis: misconceptions were captured but split, one weak entry per wrong option ("believes
  7/12", "believes 0.7"), and tutors invented a new concept id per check. That is what the hybrid
  grouping and the concept-reuse rule above fix.
- **A run with both fixes is still pending**: it was started and not yet read. These results are
  live but small (n ≤ 12 answers per arm) and are not evidence of learning gains yet.

## Open issues and notes for later slices

- **Existing bug, seen in the browser**: marking the answered card's text (`useTutorSession.answer`
  → `mutateElement`) on an old board raised Excalidraw's dev-only "Fractional indices invariant"
  error and stopped the feedback turn. It is now guarded (follow-up above); the card just isn't
  annotated in that case. The index ordering itself is not fixed.
- **Miss class from gpt-6-luna is usually below 0.5.** Keep the threshold. Its full distribution is
  in the label trace (`distributions`), and H6 or H7 could use that.
- **H6 (Constellation)**: show `suspected` misconceptions differently from `active` ones, and use
  `strength`. Reasoning tallies are in the revision's index, not in `ProfileRevision`.
- **Predict concepts**: a predict borrows the session's latest check concept (H4 rule). On an old
  board this attributed it to `linalg.determinant`, which shows up in the brief. H7's warm-up
  should prefer check evidence.
- The brief is frozen at the session's first turn. Turning Memory on later compiles it then.
- End session is in the composer rather than the toolbar, so both shells get it without touching
  their toolbars.
- `GET /learning/profile` is ready to be H6's data source: revision, beliefs, misconceptions,
  notes.
