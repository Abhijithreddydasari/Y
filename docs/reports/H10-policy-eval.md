## Caveats

- The student is simulated. A gain here is evidence that the policy teaches simulated misconceptions better, not

## Reading of this result (written by hand after the run, 2026-11-03)

- **The gate did its job.** GEPA's best candidate scored 0.745 → 0.832 on the 6 validation scenarios, but
  −0.020 on the 9 held-out scenarios (18 paired rollouts). Promoting it on the validation number would have
  shipped a slightly worse tutor. Both judges agree it is no better (Spark Open −0.020, gpt-6-luna +0.007).
- **Noise is about as large as the bar.** The same baseline prompt on the same held-out scenario differed by a
  mean of about 0.05 between its two repeats (up to 0.185). A 6-scenario, 1-repeat validation set cannot tell a
  0.05 gain from luck, so GEPA's selection overfit it.
- **Why the candidate lost.** It rewrote only `feedback_policy`: after a correct answer, ask another forced
  choice with fresh numbers. The simulated students rarely held their belief by turn 1: 120 of 157 turn-1
  answers were correct and only 10 were wrong. So the "Correct" branch drove the result. On held-out it produced
  calculation drills that drifted away from the misconception: Tomás's conv-parameter checks never varied the
  image size, and the homography checks tested plane-specificity instead of parallel lines. The judge's
  critiques name exactly this.
- **Where it helped.** On hidden misconceptions the tutor was not told about, the candidate's checks more
  often carried the wrong idea as an option: Pauli 0 → 1 on both repeats, and FlashAttention 0 → 1 on one.
- **Harness limits found.** The student gives up its belief too easily, so "resolved" is 1.0 everywhere and
  does not separate prompts. The valid rate and length parts sit at full marks too. A stronger test needs a
  stubborner student, more validation scenarios, and 2 or more repeats inside GEPA.
- **Side finding about the live tutor.** In the baseline's FlashAttention example, the `personalization` rule
  made the tutor confront an unrelated brief misconception ("fixing the equal-weights idea") in a question
  about FlashAttention.
